# Channel Estimation in MIMO Non-Orthogonal Multiple Access Systems with Pilot and CP Aid [Attached MATLAB Code]

Introduction Anticipating future vertical market adaptations in wireless communication systems for various application types, not only do we need to increase data rates but also ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC), asynchronous multi-access, and heterogeneous network architectures. Orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) faces difficulties in adapting to different application needs [1]. Therefore, both academic and industrial research has intensified the study of alternative waveforms [2], [3], [4]. Despite this, OFDM has been chosen for early versions of 5G standardization due to the need for further testing and hardware-in-the-loop experiments with non-orthogonal waveforms [6], [7].
One important aspect of future wireless communication is multi-input multi-output (MIMO) transmission. Compared to the simple and low complexity implementation of OFDM, receiver design for MIMO non-orthogonal multicarrier becomes more challenging because the receiver must handle multiple dimensions of interference, namely inter-symbol interference (ISI), inter-carrier interference (ICI), and inter-antenna interference (IAI). Multi-dimensional interference affects the reference signal received (referred to as pilots), which poses challenges to channel estimation (CE) and subsequent equalization (EQ) at the receiver, especially in broadband type communication with dual-dispersion channels. On the other hand, good time-frequency localization of pilots in non-orthogonal waveforms allows for reuse of energy from cyclic prefix (CP) pilots.
In [8] and [9], the idea of using CP information for enhanced channel estimation through Kalman filters was studied. However, their methods were only applicable to orthogonal waveforms. In this work, we leverage symbol timing localization within non-orthogonal waveforms to improve CE performance by reusing pilot energy from CP. In other words, if we properly position the pilot energy at the end of blocks, it will also include high energy when replicated into CP. Unlike [8] and [9], this work proposes any interference-limited linear modulation method, including its MIMO applications.
For the detection of non-orthogonal multicarrier data symbols, two independent CE and EQ modules are typically employed. Although numerous algorithms for joint channel estimation and equalization for MIMO-OFDM exist in literature, such as [10] and [11], they do not directly apply to non-orthogonal multicarriers due to their inability to account for multiple dimensions of interference, namely ISI, ICI, and IAI. In our previous work on CE [12], [13], we derived least squares (LS) and linear minimum mean square error (LMMSE) estimations for non-orthogonal waveforms assuming that ISI and ICI cause additional correlated noise above white noise.
As far as we know, there is no direct channel estimation solution in the literature that can be applied directly to MIMO interference-constrained non-orthogonal waveforms and also utilizes its CP information. Our contributions in this paper are threefold:
·We extend our previous work [12], [13] by deriving an LMMSE parallel interference cancellation (PIC) for MIMO non-orthogonal multicarriers that is widely accepted and close to optimal. In our derivations, we consider the pilot information across the entire block, including its CP. For non-orthogonal receivers, the pilot information is contaminated by data symbols sent over the same subcarrier. The pollution from data symbols is difficult to remove because the interference statistics are related to the channel statistics. Therefore, to derive an MIMO LMMSE estimator with a listener-like computation of interference statistics, we propose a new technique to simplify the equivalent channel model. Next, focusing on GFDM waveforms and their block cyclic structure, we derive an order LMMSE (SLMMSE) estimation version of the channel with reduced complexity.
·We thoroughly analyze the complexity of each algorithm by computing floating point operations (FLOPs). Comparing the complexity of the proposed algorithms allows us to distinguish and find a compromised algorithm suitable for different applications. For example, in URLLC, fast and lower complexity transmissions generally outperform high data rates, while in applications like video streaming, the complexity is less significant.
· Through extensive simulations, we evaluated the performance of each algorithm under different scenarios. Given the selection of modulation and coding schemes (MCS), we differentiated the most advantageous algorithms based on complexity or robustness. In our simulations, MIMO-OFDM transmission was considered as a baseline for comparison.
Article illustration
Conclusion This paper proposes a joint channel estimation and equalization technique for pilot and CP assistance in MIMO non-orthogonal waveforms. We further investigated algorithms for interference-constrained MIMO GFDM systems, with a focus on complexity. Simulation results indicate that low-complexity methods, namely SLMMSE and CP-SLMMSE, achieve nearly identical performance, despite their varying degrees of complexity and robust MCS. However, for high-order MCS, ignoring interference leads to significant performance degradation, necessitating the use of PIC. We have observed that CP-LMMSE-PIC, achieving optimal FER performance compared to an OFDM system, has a considerable SNR gap, despite further complexity. Based on FER results and complexity comparisons, we conclude that CP-SLMMSE is most suitable for robust MCS, regardless of mobility, while CP-LMMSE should be chosen for intermediate MCS, such as 16-QAM 2/3, regardless of mobility. CP-LMMSE-PIC must be selected for high data rate transmission, specifically 64-QAM 3/4.
